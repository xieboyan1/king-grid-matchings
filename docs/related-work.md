# Earlier results and the scope of the board count

Search date: November 25, 2026. The question was whether an earlier source gives the same counts, including the possibility that it describes the board under a different name.

## The square conjectures

Dawid Ryłko’s OEIS entry A401673 records numerical rows for $0\le n\le10$ and conjectures the last two entries, $1$ and $n^2(n+1)$, for squares of side $2n$ (the penultimate entry exists for $n\ge1$). His computation note explicitly describes the two patterns as observations. A011379 records the numerical sequence $n^2(n+1)$ in many contexts. Identifying the sequence does not establish that it counts these matchings. ## The all-diagonal case is already implied by earlier work

Color the board as a checkerboard. Diagonal edges stay within a color, and reflection across the vertical midline exchanges the two colors. Each component is the adjacency graph of a *mixed Aztec rectangle* after rotating and rescaling the lattice. In the usual construction one starts with the white cells of a $(2m+1)\times(2n+1)$ board with black corners and joins cells touching at a corner. Deleting the white cells on two adjacent boundary sides leaves precisely the white cells of the even rectangle. Ciucu gives the checkerboard description explicitly for mixed Aztec diamonds. Lee defines the rectangular region in §6, Figure 14(b), and explicitly observes that it has one domino tiling for every positive pair of dimensions. Each color therefore contributes one matching, proving $A_0(m,n)=1$. An empty board contributes the empty matching. [Ciucu, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.4500#page=12) · [Lee, §6, Figure 14](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v29i2p41/pdf#page=25)

There is also a graph-theoretic deduction independent of the region picture. The diagonal graph is the direct product $P_{2m}\times P_{2n}$. Brešar et al., Corollary 2.11, give a total dominating sequence as long as the number of vertices. Each step $v_i$ must discover a fresh neighbor $u_i$. Since the sequence has full length, every step discovers exactly one. Row $v_i$ of the adjacency matrix has a one in column $u_i$ and zeros in all later columns $u_j$. In these row and column orders the matrix is triangular with ones down the diagonal. Exactly one permutation maps each vertex to a neighbor. Its inverse has the same property because the graph is undirected. Uniqueness makes it an involution, and the absence of loops makes each orbit a pair. This deduces the all-diagonal result from a preprint first posted in 2017. [Brešar et al., Corollary 2.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.08780#page=7)

## The one-strip ingredient of the two-edge count

Inside a two-column strip with vertical edges forbidden, write $\pi(i)$ for the row in the right column matched to row $i$ on the left. This is a permutation with $|\pi(i)-i|\le1$. Diaconis, Graham and Holmes describe these *Fibonacci permutations* in §2.4. Propositions 2.1 and 2.4 identify the parts of sizes one and two and give $\binom{N-k}{k}$ permutations of length $N$ with $k$ adjacent transpositions. Taking $N=2m$ and $k=m-1$ gives exactly $\binom{m+1}{2}$ for one strip with two horizontal edges. Ahrens also gives the corresponding binomial count in §6.8.1, Proposition 18. [Diaconis, Graham and Holmes, 2001, §2.4](https://doi.org/10.1214/lnms/1215090070) · [Authors’ full text](https://fanchung.ucsd.edu/ron/papers/01_07_permutations.pdf) · [Ahrens, 2020](https://repository.lib.ncsu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/c9510af3-e007-4c66-88e5-028f5973bbcd/content)

For $A_2$ the additional steps concern the full board. Parity forces both axial edges to be parallel. Matching across successive pairs of columns then forces the strip decomposition. There are $n$ choices of the exceptional strip in the horizontal case. Transposing accounts for the vertical case. The resulting rectangle formula is an elementary application of the earlier one-strip enumeration. Lee’s proof of Theorem 17 also describes completions of a mixed Aztec rectangle with *prescribed* holes via Delannoy paths. The two-edge board formula sums over pairs of axial edges, whose endpoints are coupled across the two colors. We did not locate that summed formula in these sources. [Lee, §6, Theorem 17](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v29i2p41/pdf)

## Other close matches and limits of the search

Knuth studies the same diagonal graph and counts spanning trees. The earlier OEIS array A239264 counts all matchings covering a rectangular king’s grid, without sorting by edge type. Sandvik and Moessner study square-lattice nearest- and next-nearest-neighbor dimers, with an emphasis on correlations. Ryłko’s separate crossing polynomial tracks crossings between dimers. A crossing requires two particular diagonals in one unit square. Counting crossings and counting all diagonal edges give different coefficients. [Knuth](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9501234) · [A239264](https://oeis.org/A239264) · [Sandvik and Moessner](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0507277) · [Ryłko’s crossing polynomial](https://github.com/dawidrylko/lattice-dimers/blob/b8b18c3982fc5404ef793c545e1dabeb26f10bee/research/crossing-polynomial/README.md#L88)

The search followed the linked OEIS entries and the proposer’s public notes, plus indexed papers on king graphs, diagonal dimers, path direct products, mixed Aztec rectangles and bounded-displacement permutations. Search terms included `A401673 proof`, `domicule diagonal pieces`, `king graph perfect matchings two horizontal edges`, `mixed Aztec rectangle unique domino tiling`, and `Fibonacci permutations fixed points adjacent transpositions`. Earlier work settles the zero-edge case and the single-strip formula. The whole-board two-edge sum has an explicit proof here. Its priority remains open to correction from unindexed sources or differently phrased results.
